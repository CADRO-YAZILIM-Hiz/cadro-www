Is Time-Tracking Data Personal Data? A Legal Assessment

Summary: Entry, shift, break, and attendance records linked to an identifiable employee will generally be personal data. The assessment depends on linkability, purpose, and use—not simply whether the system uses QR or GPS.

Data controller and processor

The employer is generally the data controller when it decides why and how time records are processed. A software provider may act as processor under instructions and contract. Roles must match technical access and agreements.

Purpose limitation

Attendance and payroll verification may be defined purposes. Continuous profiling, unrelated behavior tracking, and reuse outside the stated purpose need separate assessment and safeguards.

Biometrics are different

Not every time record is biometric data. Fingerprint and facial recognition create a distinct, higher-risk assessment. Less intrusive alternatives should be evaluated first.

Conclusion

Time-tracking data becomes personal data through its link to an employee and processing context. A safe system limits purpose, access, retention, and makes employee rights visible.
