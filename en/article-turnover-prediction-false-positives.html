Managing False Positives in Employee Turnover Prediction

Summary: Attrition prediction can provide an early signal, but a high-risk output is not a fact. False positives can affect employee trust, manager behavior, and perceived fairness.

What is a false positive?

A false positive occurs when the model predicts that an employee will leave but the employee does not. It may trigger an unnecessary intervention or an unfair impression about the employee.

Why accuracy is insufficient

When actual departures are rare, a model can achieve high accuracy by predicting that almost everyone will stay. Precision, recall, specificity, calibration, and group-level error rates should be reviewed together.

Five controls

  1. Define the score threshold that triggers action.
  2. Check whether probabilities are calibrated.
  3. Compare errors across role, location, tenure, and team groups.
  4. Require human validation before intervention.
  5. Prohibit labeling or punishment based solely on a score.

Use a support signal, not an automated verdict

The output can prompt a respectful conversation about workload, development, management support, and working conditions. It should never be presented as “the model says you are a risk.”

Monitoring and challenge

Keep scores access-controlled, purpose-limited, and no longer than needed. Employees should understand relevant use and have a meaningful route to question incorrect information.

Conclusion

Turnover prediction is a constrained decision-support signal, not a judgment about a person. Making false positives visible improves analytical quality and fairness.
