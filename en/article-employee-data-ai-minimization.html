Data Minimization When Building AI Models with Employee Data

Summary: More data does not automatically make an HR model better. Data minimization limits purpose, fields, retention, and access as part of system design.

Why minimization affects model quality

Unnecessary data increases privacy risk and may help a model learn noise, proxies, and historical bias. Before development, inventory every field and document its purpose and contribution.

Five lifecycle controls

  1. Document purpose, legal basis, and source.
  2. Remove unnecessary fields and restrict access.
  3. Test representation, proxy features, and bias.
  4. Operate human review, logging, and challenge routes.
  5. At the end of retention, delete, destroy, or appropriately anonymize.

Deletion, destruction, and anonymization

The Turkish DPA guide explains that when the reason for processing ends, data should be deleted, destroyed, or anonymized. These are distinct outcomes: inaccessible to relevant users, inaccessible to everyone and unusable, or no longer linkable to an identifiable person even through reasonable matching.

Conclusion

Data minimization is a joint product, data, HR, security, and legal decision. Less data that is better governed supports lower-risk and more explainable HR AI.
