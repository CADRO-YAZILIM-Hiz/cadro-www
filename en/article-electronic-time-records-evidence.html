The Evidentiary Value of Electronic Time and Attendance Records

Summary: Electronic time records can be important evidence, but their value depends on how they were generated, preserved, corrected, challenged, and compared with other evidence.

Record versus actual work

An entry event does not by itself prove work throughout the period or overtime for every minute. Attendance data should be assessed with shifts, breaks, tasks, payroll, and witness evidence.

Technical reliability

  • Reliable timestamps and time zones.
  • Unique event IDs and replay controls.
  • Access, correction, and change logs.
  • Authorized users and least privilege.
  • Integrity and transfer-error records.

Corrections and challenges

A correction should preserve old value, new value, reason, user, time, and approver. Employees should be able to view relevant records, report errors, and understand how their explanation was assessed.

Conclusion

The value of electronic attendance data comes from a reliable creation process, visible history, and meaningful challenge route—not from being digital alone.
