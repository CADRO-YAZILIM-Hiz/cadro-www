Pay Equity Analytics and Explainable Salary Bands

Summary: Pay equity analytics is not only a comparison of average salaries. It tests whether differences are explainable and correctable in the context of role, level, experience, location, performance, and working conditions.

Equality and fairness

Equality asks whether comparable work receives comparable treatment. Fairness asks whether differences can be explained by legitimate, consistently applied criteria. Analysis should not declare every difference discrimination, but it should surface unexplained differences.

Define the data first

  • Job family and level.
  • Base pay, variable pay, and benefits.
  • Location, work model, and tenure.
  • Performance method and period.
  • Missing, stale, or incomparable records.

Explainable salary bands

Each band’s minimum, midpoint, and maximum should be connected to role level, skills, responsibility, and progression criteria. Managers should be able to explain a range using data and policy.

Five analytical controls

  1. Build valid comparison groups.
  2. Separate raw gaps from legitimate explanatory factors.
  3. Show sample size and uncertainty.
  4. Test by role, level, location, and time.
  5. Record owner, action, and remeasurement date.

Privacy and human review

Pay analysis can be financially and personally sensitive. Use role-based access, avoid unnecessary individual detail, and use results to support human review rather than automatic pay decisions.

Conclusion

Explainable salary bands provide a consistent progression logic, not just a number. Analytical quality depends on valid data, comparison, uncertainty, and a measurable correction plan.
